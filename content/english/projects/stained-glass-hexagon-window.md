## Content

This was my first foray into stained glass, combining woodworking and glasswork into a single challenging project. The goal was to create a decorative hexagonal window using the copper foil (Tiffany) technique.

### Materials

- Stained glass sheets in various colors
- Copper foil tape
- Lead-free solder
- Flux
- Glass cutter and running pliers
- Wood for hexagonal frame
- Glass grinder

### Build Process

The project started with cutting a hexagonal wooden frame. Getting the 60-degree angles precise was one of the most difficult parts - small errors compounded quickly when assembling six sides. Several attempts were made before achieving a frame that closed properly.

For the glass design, I used a printed pattern to guide the cuts. Each piece was scored with a glass cutter and then carefully broken along the score line. The pieces were then ground to fit snugly together before being wrapped in copper foil.

Once all pieces were foiled, they were soldered together using flux and lead-free solder. The soldering process requires a steady hand and consistent temperature to create smooth, even seams.

### Results

The finished window features a geometric pattern that catches light beautifully. While not perfect, it was a great learning experience in both frame construction and the copper foil technique. The challenging hexagonal geometry taught important lessons about precision that carried forward to later stained glass projects.

### Lessons Learned

The biggest takeaway was the importance of accuracy in the frame construction. With a hexagon, any angle error multiplies by six when you try to close the shape. Future projects would benefit from more precise measuring tools or a jig to ensure consistent angles.
