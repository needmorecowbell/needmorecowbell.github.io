After years of homebrewing and dealing with bottles, I finally decided to build a proper kegorator. The project evolved from a simple keg fridge conversion into a full bartop build that took about three weeks from start to finish.

## The Kegorator Conversion

<div style="text-align:center">
    <img src="{{<s3cdn>}}/projects/kegorator/08_20_initial.jpg" style="max-width: 600px;" loading="lazy">
    <p><em>Starting the conversion - getting the mini fridge ready for its new life.</em></p>
</div>

The base is a standard mini fridge that I modified to fit a corny keg. The conversion itself isn't too complicated - drill a hole for the tap shank, add some insulation adjustments, and run the CO2 lines. The tricky part is making sure everything fits and the temperature stays consistent.

## Building the Bartop

The real project was the bartop. I wanted something that would look good in the space and provide a proper surface for pouring.

<div style="text-align:center">
    <img src="{{<s3cdn>}}/projects/kegorator/08_22_bartop_progress.jpg" style="max-width: 600px;" loading="lazy">
    <p><em>The bartop taking shape - getting the dimensions right to fit over the kegorator.</em></p>
</div>

I built the frame to overhang the fridge slightly on all sides, giving it more of a bar feel. The surface needed to be durable enough to handle condensation from cold glasses and the occasional spill.

## What I Learned

A few takeaways from this build:

1. **Measure twice, cut once** - especially important when the bartop needs to fit precisely over the fridge
2. **Seal everything** - the bartop sees a lot of moisture, so proper sealing is essential
3. **Plan for CO2 access** - you need to be able to reach the regulator and tank without disassembling everything

The kegorator has been running great since completion. There's something satisfying about pouring a proper pint from your own tap, especially when you built the whole setup yourself.
