During the Hickman House buyout process in spring 2022, the kitchen island had seen better days. The butcher block top had accumulated iron stains - dark discolorations that made the whole piece look tired and neglected. Mom and I decided to tackle it together over Mother's Day weekend.

## The Project

The plan was straightforward: sand down the surface to remove the stains, then apply a stain and sealer combination to protect the wood going forward. We'd done similar refinishing projects before, and this seemed like a perfect weekend task.

## What We Used

We opted for a stain/acrylic sealer combination product - one of those all-in-one solutions that promises to do both jobs at once. In theory, this should have been more efficient than applying separate stain and sealer coats. In practice... well, we learned some things.

## The Process

Mom and I sanded the top to remove the iron stains and prepare the surface. The sanding went well - we got the wood back to a clean, uniform state. Then we applied the stain/sealer, working in sections to keep a wet edge.

## Lessons Learned

Here's where things got interesting. The finish remained tacky for multiple days. Not "dry to the touch in 4 hours" as promised - actually sticky when you pressed your hand against it. When it did finally cure, it had a glossier sheen than we expected.

In hindsight, the combination stain/sealer product was probably not the best choice for a horizontal work surface that sees daily use. These products can be convenient, but they don't always deliver the durability of a proper multi-coat system with dedicated stain followed by a polyurethane topcoat.

The finish eventually did cure and has held up fine, but if I were doing it again, I'd use separate products: an oil-based penetrating stain for color, then several coats of wipe-on polyurethane for protection. Takes longer, but gives you more control over the final result.

## The Takeaway

Sometimes the quick solution costs you time in the end. That said, spending a weekend with Mom working on a project together was worth any minor frustrations with the finish. The island looks dramatically better than it did with those iron stains, and the slightly-glossier-than-planned surface has actually grown on us.

Not every project goes exactly as planned, but the ones you do with family are always worth it.
