In February 2021, a few of us got together at the Hickman House for what we dubbed "The Rum Run" - an ambitious project to brew and distill our own rum from scratch. Oscar, Ammar, Alex, and I spent the weekend turning molasses into something considerably more interesting.

## The Crew

This was very much a collaborative effort. Oscar brought the equipment knowledge, Ammar helped with the fermentation science, Alex kept things organized (and entertaining), and I provided the space and enthusiasm. There's something special about learning a craft alongside friends - everyone brings different skills to the table, and the inevitable mistakes become shared memories rather than personal failures.

## The Process

### Brewing the Wash

Rum starts its life as a sugar wash - basically a fermented molasses solution. We mixed blackstrap molasses with water to get the right specific gravity, pitched our yeast, and let nature do its thing for about a week. The smell during fermentation was... distinctive. Let's call it "aggressively funky."

### Distilling

The actual distillation is where things get both technical and slightly nerve-wracking. You're essentially separating alcohol from water based on their different boiling points, while being careful to discard the foreshots and heads (which contain methanol and other compounds you definitely don't want to drink).

We ran the still carefully, collecting the hearts - the good stuff - in mason jars. Watching clear liquid transform into proper rum is genuinely magical.

## The Results

We ended up with two varieties: raw white rum and a spiced version we infused with vanilla, cinnamon, and other spices. The raw rum had a clean, slightly sweet character with that unmistakable molasses backbone. The spiced version was immediately drinkable - warm and complex.

## Lessons Learned

Home distilling taught me a lot about patience and precision. Unlike brewing beer where you have some margin for error, distilling demands attention to detail. Temperature control matters. Timing matters. And having friends who know what they're doing matters even more.

Would I do it again? Absolutely. There's a particular satisfaction in sipping something you made yourself, especially when the memories of making it include good company and plenty of laughs. The Rum Run of 2021 remains one of my favorite collaborative projects.
