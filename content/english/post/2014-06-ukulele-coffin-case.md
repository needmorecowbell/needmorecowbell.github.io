After building a couple of [electric ukuleles](/projects/making_ukuleles/) in 2014, I needed somewhere safe to keep them. But a boring rectangular case just wasn't going to cut it - so I designed a coffin-shaped case instead.

## The Design

I spent some time sketching out the plans on graph paper, working out the dimensions to fit the ukulele body snugly while maintaining that classic coffin silhouette. The main body would be about 72cm tall, 35cm wide at the shoulders, tapering down to 17cm at the foot.

## The Details

What makes this design a bit unusual is the ventilation system I planned out. The lid has 8 holes connected to ball valves - 4 for intake and 4 for exhaust. The idea was to run 12 feet of 1/8" nylon tubing to allow controlled airflow. This would let me regulate humidity inside the case to protect the wood from drying out or getting too damp.

It's probably overkill for a ukulele case, but I figured if I was going to build something custom, I might as well go all in on the engineering.

## Why a Coffin?

Partly because it's a fun aesthetic that matches the slightly quirky nature of a homemade electric ukulele. Partly because the tapered shape actually works well for instrument cases - wider at the top for the body and headstock, narrower at the bottom for the neck end. And partly because I just thought it would be cool to walk into a jam session with a tiny coffin under my arm.

Sometimes the best projects are the ones that make you smile every time you use them.
